# Q1. Is CTDI an FAA-certified dispatch system, or usable as one?

No, and it makes no such claim. CTDI is decision-support only. It is not a substitute for a certificated aircraft dispatcher, a director of operations, or any element of operational control under an operator's certificate. It fuses public federal data, scores it against explicit published thresholds, and pushes the result to the humans who hold authority. No regulatory claim of any kind is made anywhere in these materials.

# Q2. Why anchor the scoring engine to Part 135.609, and what is the regulatory exposure?

Part 135.609 HEMS VFR weather minimums (ceiling ≥ 1,000 ft, visibility ≥ 3 SM, wind ≤ 30 kt) are used solely as a published, conservative, federal threshold reference — chosen because they are public and defensible. Using them does not make CTDI a Part 135 operation and does not imply FAA endorsement. The exposure to manage is marketing conflation, and these materials manage it by stating the decision-support disclaimer explicitly and repeatedly. Formal regulatory counsel review of the positioning has not been evidenced — needs founder input.

# Q3. What exactly is your FAA SWIM status, and is it real?

Real and verified writing live. All six FAA SWIM data services are provisioned and credentialed under a real, approved subscription: FDPS, STDDS, TFMS, TBFM, ITWS, and AIM/FNS. On 2026-08-24 they were confirmed writing real data — 839,101 flight events (32,459 in the prior 24 h), 120,071 surface-movement events, 24,588 NAS program records, 35,615 TBFM arrival sequences, and 5,424 NOTAMs across 308 facilities. Ingestion is a real Solace PubSub+ subscriber, six separate sessions with their own durable queues, reconnect handling, and backpressure — not a REST wrapper. This is native SWIM push consumption, which is the hard, unglamorous engineering most small vendors skip.

# Q4. Are all six SWIM feeds always running? Be specific about availability.

No — and this is the single most important honesty point for an ops audience, so it is stated in full. The platform runs on one Raspberry Pi 5 on residential power and ISP. An automatic thermal/load governor deliberately sheds ingest containers under CPU, thermal, or local-LLM contention and restores them when pressure clears. A shed feed shows as cleanly stopped (`inactive`, exit 0), not failed — by design. At the 2026-08-24 check the measured cadence was 10 shed/restore ("LOCKDOWN") cycles in about 32 hours, each roughly 9–11 minutes, all triggered by local-LLM contention rather than heat or raw load, and every one restored cleanly and automatically. The restore path has been verified end-to-end repeatedly. So: feeds are governed and duty-cycled under contention, not always-on. There is no SLA. A stopped ingest unit is normal designed behavior, surfaced honestly by the platform's own 19-feed freshness registry — not a silent failure. Governor calibration tuning and true multi-node redundancy are documented, priced roadmap items.

# Q5. How does feed redundancy and failover actually work?

Every push feed stamps a heartbeat every 30 seconds. Before each REST poll of a push-primary source, the fetch loop checks whether push is fresh (within 90 seconds) and skips the redundant REST call when it is — push primary, REST fallback. A separate guardrail timer (every 5 minutes) force-refreshes a feed only on a genuine double failure (push unhealthy AND REST also stale beyond 4× its interval). On reconnect after any outage, a backlog-triage mechanism fast-forwards past stale queue content (older than a 2-hour floor) rather than replaying dead data into the parsers. Honest limits: TFR and NAS have no push primary and always REST-poll; and Amtrak (rail, not aviation) currently has a single live path with no automatic failover — a documented gap, disclosed because it is real, though it does not touch the SWIM aviation feeds.

# Q6. How accurate is your on-time / delay data, really?

More accurate than a typical tracker, and honestly sparse today. Since 2026-08-20 CTDI durably captures TFMS's genuine airline-reported OOOI times (out/off/on/in) alongside TFMS's own original scheduled departure and arrival, and computes delay as actual-vs-authoritative-schedule — a real measurement, not the estimate-vs-estimate proxy most products surface. A 14-day on-time history per flight number and a delay-drift flag (oldest-third vs. newest-third average delay, past a 10-minute default) are exposed on the watchlist API. The honest caveats, all disclosed by the API itself: capture is watchlist-gated (history accumulates only on days a flight was watched, not universally); there is no backfill and none is possible (nothing retained OOOI before 2026-08-20), so the dataset is young — the verification pass found 2 matched records — and the API returns "insufficient data" rather than guessing; and drift needs at least 3 matched records before it reports. The mechanism is deployed and regression-tested (including a synthetic 5→28-minute progression it correctly flagged as +20 minutes). It is a real differentiator whose value compounds with time and usage, presented as such.

# Q10. What happens if the hardware fails? What is the backup/DR posture?

A real, unremediated gap. No backup or DR system is evidenced for the SQLite database, and the platform runs on a single Raspberry Pi 5 on residential power and ISP — power and ISP are the availability floor. What does exist and was verified: an automatic thermal/load governor with a confirmed self-restore path (10 clean cycles in ~32 h at the check), and a hardened root-scope watchdog that restarts only genuinely-failed containers behind a debounce and no longer conflicts with the governor's deliberate load-sheds (a real conflict was found and fixed across three rounds of live incidents). But container self-healing is not database DR. Backup, DR, and a multi-node availability target are priced roadmap items, not present capabilities.

# Q11. What is the security posture?

Founder-run, bounded, non-destructive self-testing of the live production system — not a third-party audit, and no certification is claimed. Verified strengths on 2026-08-24: bearer-token-only auth where network origin grants no tier (a prior spoofable header-trust design was removed); tokens stored as SHA-256 hashes only (no plaintext column); secrets in an owner-only (0600) file with a git history clean of credentials; every admin action audit-logged across 32 endpoints with actor/IP/payload capture and 90-day retention (12 total rows two weeks earlier → thousands per day now — a demonstrably exercised control); a GPG-signed 706-file whole-tree manifest that skill containers and the inference layer refuse to run against on any mismatch, re-verified every 15 minutes; rootless containers under systemd; a CUI/PII scrub gate that blocks rather than redacts; and pre-commit/pre-push credential hooks now actually installed. Authorization was probed live and held on every check — anonymous, forged-token, malformed, and spoofed-public-origin requests all returned 403.

The honest, more-credible version of "we tested it." # Q13. What are the known security weaknesses?

Stated: (1) all testing to date is internal self-assessment — no external pentest or audit yet; (2) credential-lifecycle hygiene is open — issued tokens do not expire (expiry is implemented but never used at mint time) and one retired integration's admin token still awaits revocation; (3) the public-origin trust model depends on Cloudflare's edge header with no app-layer backstop — not exploitable in the current topology (verified adversarially), but a documented hardening candidate; (4) two deliberately-public keyless read surfaces (a coordination board; a research-read API, now credential-gated) await final founder scope decisions; (5) no vulnerability-disclosure program. None of the open items touches the production operational SWIM feeds or the credential store.

# Q15. Is there AI/ML in the go/no-go scoring?

No. The CPS score is a deterministic threshold engine — a deliberate design choice for auditability. Twenty-one local LLM models (Ollama on the Pi) are used only for narrative briefs and a Q&A assistant, never to compute the score, and the system degrades gracefully without them: when inference is unavailable, skills fall back to labeled deterministic templates. Honest disclosure: across the last 7 days' skill LLM calls, 41.7% (2,217 of 5,316) ran the deterministic fallback rather than local inference — driven by Ollama contention and the load-shedding above. The fallback is honest-by-design (labeled, monitored, alert-on-fallback). The $0 cloud-LLM figure is measured, not assumed, and is unaffected by the fallback (a template render is not a billed call).

# Q16. How is aircraft privacy (LADD / blocked aircraft) handled — and why is your position on it a differentiator?

Two distinct things. First, the platform ingests the FAA ReleasableAircraft database and the FAA LADD list and honors LADD in aircraft-registry lookups for privacy compliance. Second — and this is the differentiator an ops audience should note — the platform's own ADS-B receivers produce data that is not FAA-source-derived and therefore not LADD-bound. Every commercial flight-tracking feed obfuscates blocked/LADD-filtered aircraft even on the enterprise tier; own-RF does not. This is a documented capability advantage: paying a commercial vendor more makes blocked-aircraft visibility strictly worse, not better. The platform separately scrubs GPS coordinates, feeder IDs, and identifiers from all public artifacts by an enforced pipeline.
